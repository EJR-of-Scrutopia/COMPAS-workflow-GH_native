# Skin round three: the two-sided and cornered forms, from Param's night test

Written 2026-09-05 night from his three screenshots of the installed round-two build
(09F91904 = 88e170f): an open barrel arch (two-sided vault) and a four-cornered fan vault.
His words: "as we can see a few issues on the 2 side and the other sided shapes too. You can
see an issue with the banding and i think this is where most problems are. and for the 2 sided
vault the cap might not be one piece but two half pieces? i also think we are duplicating in
place many voussoirs? And theres a couple which are getting gaps?"

Round two was proven on lobed multi-seam fixtures; these forms exercise a different meeting
class: the RIDGE, where two rim families meet along an open line with free ends rather than
around a closed crown loop, and the four-corner cross where four families meet. Binding scope
for the round-three wave, diagnosis first.

## Finding 1: the banding, and the duplication suspicion

His reading, and the screenshots support it: near the crown of the fan vault whole courses
appear TWICE, interleaved and offset, a translucent double image of bands. Prime suspect, to
be measured not assumed: the round-two absorb (coalesce plus sub-band bites) or the end-stone
flank extensions may EMIT stones over intervals that the ordinary course tiling still tiles
itself on these forms, double-covering the flank bands; or a band is both absorbed into the
closer and emitted as its own course. The diagnosis must enumerate cells and measure overlap
directly: any two emitted cells of the same pattern whose plan polygons overlap beyond corner
welds are a duplication witness, counted and located per band and per mechanism.

## Finding 2: the ridge is not a cap

On the two-sided barrel the crown zone is a chaos of triangles. His musing: "the cap might not
be one piece but two half pieces?" The engine answer to be verified: on a barrel the top of
the field is a RIDGE LINE, not a shrinking closed loop; CapQualifies gates on a closed loop,
so no cap should fire there at all, and the closure that serves a ridge is the closer band
running along it with full-width stones, exactly the seam machinery. The diagnosis must say
what actually fires at the barrel crown today (cap, fans, closer, or ordinary bands folding),
and the fix must make the ridge read as coursed stones flowing over the crown, not a fan
zone. If a form's ridge ends at free edges, the end treatment follows the free-edge rules,
not cap rules. His "two half pieces" is answered by mechanism, not by splitting the cap: a
ridge gets ridge stones; a true apex keeps the one-polygon cap of round two.

## Finding 3: the remaining gaps

"A couple which are getting gaps": on these form classes some openings survive round two.
Diagnosis names each with its mechanism (the round-two classes: guide pinch-out, absorb
starve, plan drop; or a new ridge-specific class), and the fix closes them under the standing
acceptance: covered, at course size, no slivers.

## Acceptance

Round two's acceptance sentences carry over unchanged, now pinned on barrel-class and
four-corner fixtures as well as the lobed ones: zero duplicated stones (the overlap witness
count is zero), zero gaps beyond the sub-sliver-floor residues already adjudicated, the crown
of a barrel coursed like the rest of the field, and the harness green with round two's numbers
intact on the lobed fixtures (no regression on the forms already fixed).
